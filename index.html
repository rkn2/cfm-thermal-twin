About this twin

This combines the March 23, 2026 laser scan (Z+F FlexScan 22) with the February 20 and March 27 FLIR E50 thermal surveys, both by Shayla Hines (Penn State AE). The thermal findings are an AI-assisted first pass for Shayla to triage. Pins marked with a dashed ring were placed by Claude from the order of the photos and still need confirming. Unplaced groups are waiting for Shayla to pin them. This shared copy is a read-only snapshot taken 2026-09-25; pins and notes are edited in the team's working copy.

The roof in the 3D view comes from Shayla's drone video and photos (Feb 20, 2026), turned into a 3D model with Apple Object Capture and registered to the laser scan. Diamond markers show where each roof photo was aimed, accurate to about 0.5–1 m.

“Thermal” in the 3D view drapes the FLIR images onto the scan: 36 exterior images from 10 groups and 20 room images: the coat room (groups J and O) and the Hearth Room stove corner (groups I and L), each lined up with the laser scan by Claude from the camera's built-in visible photo or from window and vent outlines. Where the pale masonry scanned sparsely, the image sits on a simple measured model of the walls. Temperatures use the camera's settings (emissivity 0.95, reflected temperature 68 °F). Room images sit on the full-resolution scan and use their own colour scale, since rooms read 20–30 °F warmer than the outside walls. The cold cavity (N), groups A and M, and the coat-room ceiling-patch frames are not draped yet.

In the thermal view, “Difference” shows each outside wall relative to the 1.5 m of wall around it, and each room as the temperature factor f (below 0.75 means mould risk). The numbers behind it, including anomaly maps and a comparison of the two survey days, are on the team's thermal analysis page (team access only).

The 3D view is a 10-million-point copy of the full 506-million-point scan, resampled to even density (denser on the building than on the ground) with scanner edge streaks filtered out. It loads a quarter first, then adds detail; phones get the quarter only. The drawings are at 1 cm per pixel (1:100). Orientation follows the building's walls, not true north.
